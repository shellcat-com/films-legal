Best Way to Share Photos With a Group (iPhone and Android)

Shared albums, AirDrop, Quick Share and WhatsApp compared, with a pick for each situation.

Filmsera· November 16, 2026 ·11 min read

Short answer: For most mixed iPhone and Android groups, the best way to share photos with a group is a Google Photos shared album: free, on both platforms, and anyone with the link can view and add photos. All-iPhone groups can use an iCloud shared album, which keeps full resolution on iOS 27 but uses the owner’s storage. In the same room, AirDrop and Quick Share send files directly.

How to share photos with a group of iPhone and Android users

Mixed groups are the common case, and they now have two good answers.

Google Photos shared albums

  1. Install Google Photos (it runs on iPhone and Android) and create an album with a few photos in it.
  2. Tap Share and choose who to share it with, or create a link. Google’s sharing help says that anyone with the link can view the photos and add to the album, and that each person’s Google Account picture appears next to what they add. It doesn’t say whether adding works without signing in, so test the link on a phone that isn’t signed in to Google.
  3. Post the link in the group chat, or turn it into a QR code for an event.
  4. When someone saves a photo from the album, a copy lands in their own library, and Google notes that saved photos count toward their storage quota.

An album holds up to 20,000 photos or videos, according to Google’s album help page, which is plenty for any single event. The gap: Google’s help pages don’t say what resolution shared-album copies keep. To be safe, ask contributors to back up in Original quality, because Google says its Storage saver setting resizes photos above 16 MP to 16 MP and video above 1080p to 1080p. If you need untouched originals, use one of the full-quality options below.

iCloud shared albums, now open on the web

Apple changed shared albums with version 27 of its software. Its Shared Albums support page says friends and family can now join and contribute their own photos on the web, even without an Apple Account or Apple device. Albums created on version 27 or later store photos and videos in their original formats at full resolution and allow up to 100 participants, owner included, per Apple’s Shared Albums limits.

The catch is storage. Those photos, including everything participants add, count toward the album owner’s iCloud storage, unless the album is temporary: a temporary album holds up to 5,000 photos and videos and is deleted after 30 days. The best way to share photos with a group without losing quality

Compression hides in the tools people use most. Here’s what the official documentation says about each one.

  • WhatsApp: its help center on sending media says quality may be lower than the original because media files are usually compressed when shared. After attaching a photo, you can tap the HD icon and choose HD quality, but don’t treat the chat as an archive: the default video limit is 100 MB at 720p on a faster connection and 64 MB at 480p on a slower one.
  • Older iCloud shared albums: albums created on version 26 or earlier reduce photos to 2,048 pixels on the long edge and deliver video at up to 720p, per Apple’s limits page. Albums created on version 27 or later keep full resolution.
  • AirDrop: it can send the true original. In Photos, open the photo, tap Share, then Options, and turn on All Photos Data to share the original file with its edit history and metadata. Apple’s photo sharing guide notes this option works with AirDrop only.
  • Dropbox file requests: people upload files rather than chat previews, and Dropbox says anyone can send you a file, whether they have a Dropbox account or not. Uploads count against your Dropbox space, with a 2 GB per-file cap on the Basic, Plus and Family plans.
Before you share a public link

Apple notes that sharing a photo or video includes its metadata, such as date and time, location, device and captions. That’s worth a thought before a link goes to people you don’t know well.

iCloud Shared Albums vs iCloud Shared Photo Library

The names are close, and they solve different problems.

  • A shared album is for an event. You choose which photos go in, invite people with a link or an invitation, and version 27 albums allow up to 100 participants.
  • A Shared Photo Library is for a household. Apple’s Shared Photo Library guide says it lets you share photos and videos with up to five other people, the library creator provides the iCloud storage for everything, and you can join only one. All participants can add, edit and delete content, and photos can be shared automatically from the camera when you’re near other participants. It needs iOS 16.1, iPadOS 16.1 or macOS Ventura or later, with iCloud Photos turned on. That page doesn’t mention resolution, but Apple says iCloud Photos stores photos and videos in their original formats at full resolution.

For a trip or a party, use a shared album. For a couple or a family that wants one combined library for years, use the Shared Photo Library.

AirDrop and Quick Share: fastest for people in the same room

At the end of a trip, when everyone is still at the same table, nothing beats a direct transfer.

  • AirDrop works between iPhone, iPad and Mac within 30 feet (10 meters), and if you move out of range after a transfer starts, it continues over the internet, according to Apple’s AirDrop guide. To receive from someone who isn’t in your contacts, choose Everyone for 10 Minutes; on iOS 26.2 or later, a sender who isn’t in your contacts has to use an AirDrop code.
  • Quick Share runs on Android 6 and later, Chromebooks and select Windows PCs. Google’s Quick Share help says to bring devices within 1 foot (30 cm) of each other if they don’t connect. It also says Pixel 8a or later and certain Samsung, Motorola, OnePlus, Xiaomi and other phones can send straight to an iPhone, iPad or Mac, where the files arrive through AirDrop. When devices aren’t nearby, Quick Share can share through a QR code: the files stay available for 24 hours on Google’s servers, and both devices need internet access.

Both are best for a final swap at the table, not for a group of 30: each person sends their own photos, and nobody ends up with one complete album.

The best option for each situation

A trip with friends

Make a Google Photos shared album before you leave, or an iCloud shared album if everyone has an iPhone, and post the link in the trip chat on day one. On the last night, AirDrop or Quick Share the best shots to whoever is making the album. If you’d rather everyone see the whole trip at once, here’s how to run one shared film for a group trip.

A wedding

Guest counts break the usual tools. An iCloud shared album tops out at 100 participants, owner included, so a 150-guest wedding needs something else. A Google Photos link printed as a QR code works on any phone; the full setup is in our guide to a Google Photos shared album for a wedding, and every other method is in how to collect photos from wedding guests.

A family reunion

Expect mixed phones and a few relatives with no smartphone at all. Use a Google Photos shared album for collecting, then order prints or a book for the grandparents. Our family reunion photo ideas guide covers the group photo and the logistics.

A team or work event

Use the tools your workplace already manages, such as a shared Google Drive or Dropbox folder, so photos stay under company accounts. To collect files from people outside the company, a Dropbox file request takes uploads from anyone, and Dropbox keeps the destination folder private by default. Ask before posting colleagues’ photos anywhere public.

A one-night party

A group chat handles the first few photos. For the whole night, one shared camera with a reveal the next morning works better, which is where the next section comes in.

FAQ

What is the best free app to share photos with a group?

Google Photos, for most groups. It runs on iPhone and Android, sharing is free, and Google says anyone with an album link can view and add photos. If everyone has an iPhone, an iCloud shared album is just as easy and keeps full resolution on albums made with version 27 or later, though those photos count toward the owner’s iCloud storage.

Does AirDrop work with Android phones?

Not from Apple’s side: Apple describes AirDrop as working between iPhone, iPad and Mac. Google, however, says select Android phones can send files to an iPhone, iPad or Mac with Quick Share. For other phones, use a shared album link or Quick Share’s QR code option, which needs internet on both devices.

Does WhatsApp reduce photo quality?

Usually, yes. WhatsApp’s help center says media may be lower quality than the original because files are usually compressed when shared. Choosing HD quality after you attach a photo helps, but a chat is still not an archive, and the default video limit is 720p even on a fast connection. For originals, use AirDrop with All Photos Data turned on, or a file request.

Can people add photos to a shared album without an account?

It depends on the tool. Apple says iCloud shared albums on version 27 let friends and family join and contribute on the web without an Apple Account. A Dropbox file request accepts uploads from anyone, Dropbox account or not. Google’s help says anyone with an album link can add photos, but it doesn’t say whether that works without signing in, and additions show under the contributor’s Google Account, so test the link first.

How many people can join a shared photo album?

An iCloud shared album allows up to 100 participants, owner included, on albums created with version 27 or later, and older albums allow 100 subscribers. Google’s help pages cap an album at 20,000 photos or videos but don’t list a member limit. An iCloud Shared Photo Library is smaller by design: you plus five other people.
