How Long Should a Wedding Slideshow Be? The Simple Math

Minutes to photos to songs, by setting, plus a method for cutting a big pile down.

Filmsera· November 16, 2026 ·7 min read

Short answer: How long should a wedding slideshow be? If guests sit and watch it, 5 to 8 minutes: about 60 to 95 photos at 5 seconds each, or two songs. A rehearsal dinner, with a smaller and closer crowd, can stretch to about 10 minutes. A slideshow that loops in the background during cocktail hour can run as long as you like.

The right length depends less on how many photos you love and more on whether people are expected to watch from start to finish. Below are the numbers for each setting, a table that converts minutes into photos and songs, and a method for cutting a 400-photo pile down to the 80 or so that make it.

Wedding planning sites land in the same range. WeddingWire’s guide to a wedding photo slideshow suggests about five minutes for a set presentation and calls 60 photos a good rule of thumb, while a background slideshow can hold as many as you like. The Knot’s slideshow guide puts the average sit-down slideshow at around 30 photos or under five minutes, and the planner it quotes says one playing in the background can run longer. Wedding Spot recommends 5 to 10 minutes, or 60 to 120 photos.

Why the sit-down version stays short: the room stops while it plays. Conversations pause, the bar slows down and the people at the back crane to see. Eight minutes of that feels generous; fifteen feels like a delay.

How many photos for a wedding slideshow?

Multiply the minutes by 60, then divide by the seconds each photo stays on screen. A 7-minute slideshow at 5 seconds per photo is 7 × 60 ÷ 5 = 84 photos.

How many photos per song?

Divide the song’s length in seconds by the seconds per photo. A 3½-minute song (210 seconds) holds 52 photos at 4 seconds, 42 at 5 seconds or 35 at 6 seconds. Real songs vary, so check yours:

How long should each photo stay on screen?

  • 5 seconds is the standard. It’s long enough to recognize faces and short enough to keep moving. It’s also PowerPoint’s default: Microsoft’s guide to turning a presentation into a video gives 5 seconds to any slide without its own timing.
  • 6 or 7 seconds for group photos and old prints, so people can find themselves and their grandparents.
  • 2 to 3 seconds for a fast montage set to an upbeat song, cut on the beat.
  • Captions and title cards should stay up long enough to read twice, about 3 seconds for a short title. Keep the words short rather than holding the slide longer.

What should change the length?

  • When it plays. Right before dinner is served, guests are hungry; right after the toasts, they’re already seated and settled. If the toasts run long, shorten the slideshow, not the other way around.
  • Sightlines. If half the tables can’t see the screen well, go shorter, or switch to a loop people can walk up to.
  • Sound. If the speakers can’t carry the song clearly, a long show turns into silent photos. Test the audio, and cut harder if it’s weak.
  • The crowd. A rehearsal dinner of family and close friends will sit through more than a ballroom of 200.
  • Two versions. You don’t have to choose. Show a tight 6-minute cut once, and run the longer version on a loop during cocktail hour or at the brunch.

How to cut 400 photos down to 80

  1. Set the length first. Seven minutes at 5 seconds a photo gives you 84 slots. That number is the budget.
  2. Split the budget by chapter, for example 25 growing up, 12 before you met, 25 together, 8 for the proposal and 14 for family and friends.
  3. Do a fast first pass. Give each photo about 3 seconds and mark it yes or no. At that pace, 400 photos take about 20 minutes.
  4. Collapse the near-duplicates. Five shots of the same toast become one: the one with eyes open and the best expression.
  5. Favor faces you can see from the back of the room. Tiny people in a wide landscape read as scenery on a projector.
  6. Check that everyone important appears. Parents, siblings, grandparents, the wedding party. Add a slot for anyone missing before you add another photo of the two of you.
  7. Watch it at full speed, and cut wherever you catch yourself waiting for the next photo.
  8. Keep the cuts. The extras become the cocktail-hour loop, the website gallery or a short reel later.

Short reels for sharing vs the long version for the room

The room version and the online version are different videos. The room version is landscape and runs minutes. The version people watch on a phone is vertical (9:16) and runs seconds: 15 to 60 for most stories and reels, with faster cuts.

FAQ

How many pictures should be in a 5-minute slideshow?

About 60 at 5 seconds per photo. At 4 seconds you can fit 75, and at 6 seconds about 50. Take off a few for title cards and a closing photo that holds while the music fades, so plan on 55 or so photos for a 5-minute show with one song change.

Is a 10-minute wedding slideshow too long?

For a reception where everyone stops to watch, usually yes: guides from WeddingWire, The Knot and Wedding Spot range from under 5 minutes to 10 at most. Ten minutes works at a rehearsal dinner with family and close friends, or as a loop during cocktail hour, where guests can wander off and come back. If you have 10 minutes of photos you love, show a 6-minute cut and loop the full version.

How long should a rehearsal dinner slideshow be?

About 8 to 10 minutes, or two to three songs. The crowd is smaller and knows the people on screen, so it can carry a longer show than a reception. Watch the total program, though: if the speeches already run 30 minutes, trim the slideshow to 6 or 7 minutes so the evening keeps moving.

How long should a looping cocktail hour slideshow be?

Make it at least 10 to 15 minutes so the repeat isn’t obvious to guests standing nearby. A loop can hold far more photos than a sit-down show, so this is the place for the extras, group shots and photos of guests. Export it without music or with quiet instrumentals so it doesn’t fight the room.

How many songs do you need for a 7-minute slideshow?

Two songs of about 3½ minutes each. That gives you roughly 84 photos at 5 seconds apiece. Let the change of song mark the change of chapter, such as growing up in the first song and your story together in the second, and fade the first song out over its last 2 or 3 seconds.
