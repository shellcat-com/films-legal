Short answer: The best holiday party games for adults take one sentence to explain and never require anyone to drink or perform alone. At the office, play white elephant, team trivia and a photo scavenger hunt. With friends, try fishbowl or charades. For 30 or more people, play heads or tails or a left-right gift pass. Plan three games, not ten.

Here are 30 holiday party games for adults, sorted by where you’re playing: the office, a friend’s living room, a big crowd, a mixed-age family gathering or a video call. Each one comes with the time it takes, what you need and a one-line how-to. Start with the quick-pick table, then jump to your setting.

Four rules make almost any game land:

  1. The one-sentence test. If you can’t explain it in one sentence, people will drift off during the rules.
  2. Teams beat solo play once you have more than about ten people. Nobody is on the spot, and quieter guests can contribute without performing.
  3. Plan an arc of three: a mixer as people arrive, one main event after food, and a quick closer before people start leaving.
  4. Time-box everything. Twenty minutes is plenty for most games. Stop while people are still having fun.

Holiday party games for the office

All eight work sober, in a conference room or on a lunch break, and none of them single anyone out.

  • 1. White elephant swap. 30–45 min · one wrapped gift per player, with a spending cap. Players draw numbers; on your turn, open a new gift or steal an opened one. A common house rule freezes a gift after its third steal. Budget about two minutes per player, so 20 people take roughly 40 minutes; above 25, split into two circles. Variants: a theme (“fits in a mug”), a re-gift edition, or dice rolls that decide who swaps.
  • 2. Team trivia. 30 min · a question list and answer sheets. Four rounds of five questions: winter songs, holiday movies, food traditions around the world, and “our year.” Write that last round about your own team (“Which team brought the sourdough starter in March?”). Nobody can look those up, and everyone gets to be an expert.
  • 3. Name that tune, winter edition. 15 min · a speaker and a playlist. Play the first five seconds of a song; the first team to name it scores. Mix in winter songs that aren’t tied to one holiday, like “Let It Snow” and “Winter Wonderland,” so everyone has a shot.
  • 4. Minute to win it. 20 min · cups, candy canes, oven mitts, chopsticks. One-minute challenges: stack 21 cups into a tree, move candy canes with a candy cane held in your mouth, unwrap a box in oven mitts, move ornaments into a bowl with chopsticks. Teams send a different player each round.
  • 5. Office photo scavenger hunt. 30 min · a prompt list per team. Teams of four to six race to photograph prompts like “your whole team in one frame” or “someone from a department you never visit.” Points for creativity, not speed.
  • 6. Guess the baby photo. 20 min · photos collected a week ahead, a numbered board, guess sheets. Strictly optional: only people who send a photo are in it. Delete the collection after the party.
  • 7. Ornament or desk-decor swap. 20 min · one small wrapped item each. White elephant rules with one twist: anything for a tree or a desk counts, so people who don’t have a tree can play too.
  • 8. Gingerbread build-off. 30 min · graham crackers, frosting, candy, paper plates. Teams build a house, a scene or a replica of the office. It suits quieter people, because the talking happens while hands are busy.

How to keep holiday party games office-safe

The best office games are the ones nobody has to recover from on Monday. A few rules, some straight from HR guidance, keep things comfortable:

  • Make every game optional. SHRM’s advice on inclusive holiday celebrations is not to make the party mandatory, and for managers to make clear that nothing happens to people who skip it. The same goes for each game. Cheering from the side is a fine way to play.
  • Keep it non-religious unless your team chose otherwise. The same article warns about a “holiday party” that is really a Christmas party in disguise, and suggests checking an interfaith calendar before picking a date. Winter themes, food and “our year” trivia work for everyone.
  • No drinking games at work. SHRM’s piece on recovery-friendly office parties recommends not building the party around alcohol, presenting mocktails and nonalcoholic beer as equal options, and considering a brunch or lunch instead of an evening event. It also suggests a “no alcohol gifts” rule for gift exchanges. Games should never reward drinking.
  • Skip anything that singles people out. Solo karaoke, roasts, games about bodies, salaries or dating, and “most likely to” prompts that could sting. Teams and anonymous voting keep the spotlight off individuals.
  • Offer seated options. Not every game should involve running, crouching or lifting. Trivia, the gingerbread build and the camera challenge work for everyone.
  • Ask before posting photos. Share inside the company first. Public posts need a yes from the people in them.

Holiday party games for friends at home

With friends you can be louder, sillier and a little more competitive. These seven work best with 6 to 14 people.

  • 9. Holiday charades. 20 min · slips with movie and song titles. One person acts, their team guesses within a minute. Classic for a reason.
  • 10. Fishbowl. 30–40 min · paper slips, a bowl, a timer. Everyone writes three holiday words or names. Round one: describe without saying the word. Round two: one word only. Round three: act it out. The same slips come back each round, so the jokes build.
  • 11. Most likely to, holiday edition. 15 min · a list of prompts. Read “Most likely to regift a regift” or “most likely to have their lights up by November 1,” then everyone points on three. Keep the prompts affectionate.
  • 12. Holiday Scattergories. 20 min · paper, pens, a list of categories. Roll or draw a letter; everyone has two minutes to fill categories like “a holiday food” or “something in a stocking.” Unique answers score.
  • 13. Two truths and a lie: year in review. 15 min · nothing. Each person shares three things from their year; the room votes on the lie.
  • 14. Blind cookie tasting. 20 min · four kinds of cookies, numbered plates, score cards. Guests rate taste and guess which are homemade and which are store-bought. Store-bought sometimes wins, which is half the fun.
  • 15. Describe it badly. 15 min · nothing. Someone describes a holiday movie’s plot as badly as possible (“Rich man underpays his only employee until three ghosts stage an intervention”) and the room guesses.

Holiday party games for large groups

Past about 30 people, pick games where everyone plays at once and nobody waits for a turn.

  • 16. Heads or tails. 5 min · one coin, any number of players. Everyone stands and puts hands on their head (heads) or hips (tails). Flip the coin; anyone who guessed wrong sits down. Last one standing wins. Works for 20 or 200.
  • 17. Left-right gift pass. 10 min · one small gift per circle or table. Read a short story aloud; every time you say “left” or “right,” the gifts move that way. Whoever holds a gift at the end keeps it.
  • 18. Find-someone-who bingo. 20 min · printed bingo cards. Squares like “has never seen a holiday movie” or “started at the company this year.” Guests mingle to find a different person for each square.
  • 19. Survey says, holiday edition. 30 min · a short survey sent a week ahead, a scoreboard. It plays like Family Feud. Ask guests “Name something people regift” and similar, then two teams guess the top answers on the night.
  • 20. Snowball toss icebreaker. 10 min · paper and pens. Everyone writes a fun question, crumples it and throws it across the room on “go.” Pick one up, find someone you don’t know and ask it.
  • 21. Gift-wrap relay. 15 min · boxes, paper, tape, ribbon. Pairs wrap a box using one hand each, then hand off to the next pair. The neatest and the fastest both win something.

Here’s a left-right story you can read as is:

The office was quiet RIGHT before the holidays. Then the manager LEFT a note: party at five, RIGHT after the meeting. Everyone LEFT their desks early. The new hire went RIGHT to the snacks and LEFT the cookies alone, RIGHT up until the gingerbread came out. Once the music started, nobody was LEFT sitting down. The night went RIGHT, and when it was over, everyone LEFT happy with whatever gift was RIGHT in their hands.

Family-friendly holiday games for mixed ages

When the room runs from six to 86, pick games where kids and grandparents can win.

  • 22. Holiday Pictionary. 20 min · a big pad or whiteboard, word slips. Pair a kid with an adult on each team so drawing skill evens out.
  • 23. Who am I? 15 min · sticky notes. Stick a holiday character on each forehead; players ask yes-or-no questions until they guess who they are.
  • 24. Price guess. 15 min · six grocery items with the receipt hidden. Everyone guesses each price; closest without going over wins. Everyone has a theory about the price of eggs.
  • 25. Pass the parcel. 15 min · one prize wrapped in many layers, a small treat in each layer, music. Pass it while the music plays; when it stops, the holder unwraps one layer.

Virtual and hybrid holiday party games

On a video call, keep games short, visual and playable by people with their camera off.

  • 26. A Jackbox game over screen share. 30 min · one copy of the game. Jackbox’s own remote-play guide has the host share the game screen on a video call while players join at jackbox.tv on their own phones with the room code. Many of the games have extended timers for remote players, which helps with call lag.
  • 27. Emoji movie quiz. 15 min · a list of emoji strings. Post a holiday movie as emoji in the chat; the first correct answer scores. Camera-off friendly.
  • 28. Sixty-second show and tell. 20 min · nothing. Everyone brings the strangest holiday object in their home and gets one minute to explain it.
  • 29. Grab-it scavenger hunt. 10 min · nothing. Call out “something red,” “a mug with a story,” or “the oldest thing in your kitchen,” and the first person back on camera with it scores.

For hybrid parties, give remote players a teammate in the room who relays answers, and point a laptop at the gift table so everyone can see the white elephant steals.

Game 30: the shared disposable camera challenge

This one runs in the background of all the others. Everyone shoots a few photos of the party on one shared roll, nobody can see any of them during the night, and the whole roll opens the next morning. It needs no rules speech and no stage, works for 8 people or 80, and gives the party a second moment: the next day, when everyone opens the photos together and finds out what the night looked like from every angle. If you want a winner, vote on the best shot after the reveal.

FAQ

What are good holiday party games for adults?

The ones that take a sentence to explain and work in teams: white elephant, team trivia, name that tune, minute to win it, charades, fishbowl and a photo scavenger hunt. For big crowds, heads or tails and a left-right gift pass get everyone playing at once. Pick three for one party: a mixer, a main event and a closer.

What games can you play at an office holiday party?

White elephant, team trivia with an “our year” round, name that tune with winter songs, minute to win it, a photo scavenger hunt, guess the baby photo, a desk-decor swap and a gingerbread build-off. Keep every game optional, non-religious and sober, and avoid anything that puts one person on the spot.

How do you play white elephant?

Everyone brings one wrapped gift under a set price and draws a number. Player one opens a gift. Each next player either opens a new gift or steals an opened one, and anyone who loses a gift opens a new one or steals a different one. A common house rule freezes a gift after its third steal. Allow about two minutes per player.

What are good holiday games for large groups?

Choose games where everyone plays at once. Heads or tails takes five minutes and one coin for any crowd size. A left-right gift pass works at every table, find-someone-who bingo gets people mingling, and a Family Feud-style game built from a survey of your guests works on a stage with two teams.

How many games should you plan for a holiday party?

Three is the sweet spot: a mixer as people arrive, a main event after food, and a quick closer before people leave. Give each one 15 to 30 minutes. A background game like a shared camera or bingo card can run all night alongside them without needing any extra time.
