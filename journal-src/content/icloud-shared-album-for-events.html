Short answer: Yes, if the album was created or upgraded on iOS 27 or later. Android users can then join an iCloud shared album and add photos in a web browser, and Apple says they don’t need an Apple Account or Apple device. That change arrived with Apple’s version 27 software, so many older answers to the iCloud shared album Android question are now out of date. Can you use an iCloud shared album on Android?

Yes, with one condition: the album has to use Apple’s new shared album experience, which comes with version 27 of iOS, iPadOS and macOS. Apple’s Shared Albums guide says that with it, friends and family can join and contribute their own photos on the web, even without an Apple Account or Apple device. In practice:

  • Android and Windows guests use a web browser. They open the album link and, if prompted, tap Send Request; Apple says they’re notified once the album is shared with them. Apple’s page on upgrading shared albums adds that an upgraded album can be opened on iCloud.com from any device.
  • Apple devices need version 27 too. To open an upgraded album in the Photos app, a device needs iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27 or later. Anyone on older software has to update or view the album on iCloud.com.
  • Old albums don’t change by themselves. Albums made on version 26 or earlier stay as they are unless the owner upgrades them. On those, the owner can turn on Public Website, which publishes the photos to a page anyone can view in an up-to-date browser. To let Android guests add photos too, upgrade the album.
  • Test before the event. Apple’s pages don’t walk through the exact screens an Android guest sees, so send the link to one Android phone a week ahead and add a test photo from it.

How to create a shared album for an event and invite guests

These steps are for iPhone on iOS 27, from Apple’s Shared Albums and temporary album guides.

  1. Check the settings. Go to Settings, tap your name, tap iCloud, then tap Photos under Saved to iCloud. On iOS 27, make sure iCloud Photos is on, then turn on Shared Albums. The exception is a temporary album, which doesn’t need iCloud Photos.
  2. Create the album. Open Photos, tap Collections, then Shared Albums, and tap Add.
  3. Name it and add a few photos. Use the event and the date in the name, so guests know they’ve found the right album.
  4. Pick who can get in. Under Sharing Options, choose between approving each person and letting anyone with the link join.
  5. Decide on temporary. For an event, consider turning on Make Album Temporary (see below), then tap Done.
  6. Share the link. Open the album, tap Manage Shared Album, then Invite Participants, and share or copy the link.
  7. Check permissions. Guests need Can Add Photos to contribute. If someone sees the album but can’t add, this setting is the first thing to check.

If you chose approval, each request arrives as a notification, and you tap Approve or Decline. For a sign at the venue, turn the link into a QR code with any QR generator and print it at least 2 inches wide.

Temporary or permanent: which album suits an event?

For most events, temporary is the smarter default, as long as someone saves the photos in time.

  • Temporary: nothing counts against anyone’s storage, and the album deletes itself after 30 days. Apple’s warning is blunt: any photos or videos not saved to your library will be deleted. To keep the album, open Manage Shared Album and turn off Make Album Temporary; from then on, it counts toward your iCloud storage limit.
  • Permanent: every photo and video, including the ones guests add, counts toward the owner’s iCloud storage. Apple’s iCloud+ plans page lists 5 GB free, and 50 GB for $0.99 a month in the US (as of November 2026).

Here’s why storage matters. Say 60 guests each add 25 photos: that’s 1,500 photos. If you assume about 3 MB per photo (real sizes vary by phone and settings), that’s roughly 4.5 GB, close to the whole free 5 GB before anyone adds a video. Full resolution is the upside of the new albums, and storage is the bill.

Tip

If you make a temporary album for an event, set a reminder for day 25 to save everything to your library, or to turn off Make Album Temporary if you have the storage.

How the iCloud shared album link works

On version 27 albums, the link is the invitation. Depending on your Sharing Options, anyone with it can join without approval, or people request access and you approve each one. A few things worth knowing before you print it on a sign:

  • Hand out management sparingly. Anyone with Can Manage Album and Photos can control who has access and delete anyone’s photos, just like the owner. Give it to a co-host, not the whole guest list.
  • Removing someone: tap the album, tap Manage Shared Album, select the participant, then tap Remove Participant.
  • Deleting: participants can delete only what they added. Deleting the shared album itself permanently deletes all of its photos.
  • Saving: open a photo, tap Share, then Save Image or Save Video. Saved copies stay in your library even if the album is deleted later.
  • Open links travel. If the QR code will hang where strangers can see it, such as a restaurant or a public park, use approval instead of an open link.

iCloud shared album not working? Apple’s fixes

When an album won’t show up or stops syncing, Apple’s troubleshooting page suggests working through these in order:

  1. Check that each device meets the minimum system requirements, and update to version 27 or later if you can.
  2. Check the internet connection on every device by opening apple.com or iCloud.com.
  3. Go to Settings, tap Apps, then Photos, and make sure Shared Albums is turned on.
  4. See whether the album loads on another device, at icloud.com/photos, or for another participant.
  5. Ask the owner to check they haven’t gone over their iCloud storage limit; they can free up space or upgrade their iCloud+ plan.
  6. On version 26 or earlier only: back up the album’s photos, turn Shared Albums off, wait 10 minutes and turn it back on.

Two problems have their own cause. If you can see the album but can’t add, like or comment, you’re probably on older software looking at an album made or upgraded on version 27: those appear read-only. Update the device or use the album on iCloud.com. If photos look soft, the album was probably created on version 26 or earlier, where photos are reduced to 2,048 pixels. Upgrading helps from then on: Apple says newly shared photos and videos will be in their original resolution, and it keeps a copy of the old album in Shared Albums settings for six months.

Is an iCloud shared album good for a wedding or event?

It’s an excellent choice when:

  • Most of the people adding photos have iPhones, and there are fewer than 100 of them.
  • You want full-resolution photos in one place, and you’ll either save a temporary album within 30 days or have iCloud storage to spare.
  • It’s a family holiday or a reunion where everyone already uses Photos. Our family reunion photo ideas cover the shots worth collecting.

Use something else, or something alongside it, when:

  • More than 100 guests should be able to add photos. Apple’s cap is 100 participants, owner included.
  • Many guests are on Android and you can’t test the web flow before the day.
  • You want a surprise reveal. A shared album shows photos as they’re added, and Apple says participants get a notification when new photos arrive.
  • You don’t want photos counting toward your storage and can’t commit to saving a temporary album within 30 days.

In either of the first two cases, a Google Photos shared album is the usual cross-platform alternative, since its app runs on both iPhone and Android. FAQ

Can Windows PC users join an iCloud shared album?

Yes, through a web browser. Apple says an upgraded shared album can be opened on iCloud.com from any device, and that friends and family can join and contribute their own photos on the web without an Apple Account. Older albums made on version 26 or earlier offer Public Website instead, which publishes the photos to a page anyone can see in a browser.

Do iCloud shared album photos count against my storage?

On albums you own that were created on version 27 or later, yes: Apple says the photos and videos participants add also count toward your iCloud storage. Temporary shared albums don’t count, and albums created on version 26 or earlier don’t count either. If you upgrade an old album, Apple says only the new items count.

How long does a temporary shared album last?

Thirty days. Apple says any photos or videos not saved to your library are deleted when it expires. A temporary album holds up to 5,000 photos and videos, doesn’t use iCloud storage and doesn’t need iCloud Photos turned on. To keep it, turn off Make Album Temporary in Manage Shared Album, and it then counts toward your storage.

How many people can join an iCloud shared album?

Up to 100 participants, owner included, on albums created on version 27 or later, and older albums allow up to 100 subscribers. That cap rules out an iCloud album as the only collection tool for a big wedding. If more than 100 people need to add photos, use a Google Photos link or a shared camera alongside it.

What happens to my old shared albums after iOS 27?

Nothing, unless you upgrade them. Apple says albums created on earlier software stay the same until you upgrade; after that, newly shared photos and videos are in their original resolution and new items count toward the owner’s storage. After an upgrade, Apple also keeps a copy of the old album in Shared Albums settings for six months before deleting that copy permanently.
