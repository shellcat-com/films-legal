What is a photo dump?

A photo dump is a casual multi-photo post, usually an Instagram carousel, that gathers a stretch of time (a weekend, a trip, a month) into loosely ordered snapshots instead of one polished picture. The charm is in the mix: the blurry ones sit right next to the good ones.

How many can you post? As of November 2026, an Instagram carousel holds up to 20 photos and videos, according to Meta’s developer documentation; Instagram doubled the old limit of 10 in August 2024. For TikTok photo posts, its developer documentation lists up to 35 photos. You rarely need the maximum: 8 to 12 strong slides beat 20 so-so ones.

How to order a photo dump

A dump looks effortless when the order isn’t. Six moves do most of the work:

  1. Slide 1 is the cover. It’s the photo people see first in the feed and the one on your profile grid, so use your clearest, most telling shot: faces, or a scene that explains the event at a glance.
  2. Slides 2 to 4 set the scene. A wide shot, the place, the people you were with.
  3. The middle is for variety. Alternate wide and close, people and details, photo and video. Never put two near-identical shots side by side.
  4. Drop a short clip in early. A few seconds of motion around slides 3 to 5 keeps people swiping.
  5. Save a laugh for the second-to-last slide. The chaotic or blurry one that sums up the night.
  6. End quiet, or on a callback. The walk home, the empty table, or a final photo that pays off a joke in your caption.

Two finishing touches. Crop every slide to the same shape before you post (portrait is the usual choice), so you decide what gets cut off, not the app. And give the set one look: the same filter or film look on every photo turns a random mix into one story. FAQ

What is a photo dump on Instagram?

A photo dump is a casual carousel post that gathers a weekend, trip, event or month into a loose set of snapshots rather than one polished photo. Instagram lets you share up to 20 photos and videos in one post. Captions are usually short and playful, and the mix of great and imperfect shots is the point.

How many photos should be in a photo dump?

Eight to twelve is the sweet spot for most dumps: enough to tell the story, few enough that every slide earns its place. Instagram allows up to 20 photos and videos per post, and TikTok’s developer documentation lists up to 35 photos for a photo post, but more slides only help when every one adds something new.

What should you caption a photo dump?

Keep it short and specific. Name the event, the place or the feeling in a few words, like “Last night, reconstructed” or “Lisbon, in 14 slides.” A light joke works well, and so does a question that invites comments, such as asking friends which slide is their favorite. Save the longer story for the comments.

How do you make a photo dump look aesthetic?

Give it one look and a rhythm. Edit every photo with the same filter or film look, crop them all to the same shape, and alternate wide shots with close details. Open on your strongest photo, tuck a short clip near the start, and end on something quiet or funny. Cut anything that repeats a moment you already have.

Should the first photo in a photo dump be the best one?

Yes. The first slide is what people see in their feed and on your profile grid, so it decides whether anyone swipes. Use the clearest photo that explains the event at a glance, ideally with faces. Save the blurry favorites for the middle and the funniest shot for the second-to-last slide.

Is it okay to post friends in a photo dump?

Ask first, especially for unflattering shots, anything from a night out, and anyone’s kids. Most friends are happy to be included when they get a say. Tag the people who agree, credit whoever took the photos you didn’t, and if someone asks you to take a photo down, do it quickly, even if that means reposting the whole dump.
