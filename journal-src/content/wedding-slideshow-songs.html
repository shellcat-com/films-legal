Short answer: The best wedding slideshow songs match the chapter on screen: warm, slower songs for growing up (“In My Life,” “Forever Young”), bright ones for how you met (“Lucky,” “Love Story”), singalongs for friends (“Lean on Me”) and a warm closer (“What a Wonderful World”). Plan one song for every 3 to 4 minutes of slideshow, or about 40 photos per song.

Below are 100 wedding slideshow songs grouped by chapter, with a note on the feel of each section and how fast to move the photos. Every title and artist was checked against Apple Music’s catalog in November 2026. How to choose wedding slideshow songs

  • Pick the chapter first, then the song. Baby photos, dating photos and friend-group photos each want a different feel. Our step-by-step slideshow guide shows how to split your photos into chapters.
  • Let the tempo set the pace. Slow songs can hold a photo for 5 to 7 seconds; upbeat songs want quick cuts of 2 to 3 seconds, landing on the beat.
  • Listen to every lyric, start to finish. Plenty of songs that sound romantic are about breakups or loss.
  • Mix eras. One song the grandparents know and one your friends will sing along to keeps the whole room with you.
  • Check the length of your version. The album version of “Isn’t She Lovely” runs over 6 minutes, so you may want a shorter edit or a trim.

Songs that sound romantic but aren’t

  • “Every Breath You Take” – The Police. The lyric is about watching someone obsessively.
  • “I Will Always Love You” – Whitney Houston. A goodbye song.
  • “Someone Like You” – Adele. About an ex who has moved on.
  • “Marry Me” – Thomas Rhett. The singer imagines watching the woman he loves marry someone else.
  • “Cat’s in the Cradle” – Harry Chapin. A father who missed his son’s childhood; skip it for the growing-up chapter.

How many songs does a wedding slideshow need?

Plan one song for every 3½ minutes. By our count, about two in three of the songs above run between 3 and 4½ minutes on Apple Music, so 3½ minutes is a fair planning number; the classics tend to run shorter. To fit photos to a song, divide its length in seconds by the seconds per photo: “Can’t Help Falling in Love” runs 3:06 on the Blue Hawaii album, or 186 seconds, which is 37 photos at 5 seconds each.

For the full math on length, including how long a sit-down slideshow should run, see how long a wedding slideshow should be. A few editing habits make the music feel planned:

  • Start each song on its first beat and put a chapter title there.
  • Fade out over the last 2 or 3 seconds, and crossfade a second or two into the next song.
  • Cut long songs at the end of a chorus, never in the middle of a line.
  • Even out the volume. Older recordings are often quieter than new ones, so turn the loud tracks down rather than the quiet ones up.

Can you post a wedding slideshow with copyrighted music?

Playing a song in the room and posting it online are different situations. For the room, ask your DJ or venue coordinator how they want to handle it. Online, platforms match the audio in your upload against songs registered by rights owners. On YouTube, a match creates a Content ID claim, and the owner can block the video, run ads on it or track it; Shorts between one and three minutes long are blocked while a claim is active, according to YouTube’s Content ID help page. Meta’s music guidelines for Facebook and Instagram say content with music that isn’t properly authorized may be blocked, muted or removed, and recommend shorter clips of music.

That’s why a slideshow that played fine at the reception can come back silent after you upload it. The fix is to keep the room version for the room and post a version with music you’re allowed to use.

Where to get music you can post

  • YouTube Audio Library. Free royalty-free production music inside YouTube Studio. YouTube says copyright-safe tracks from the library won’t get Content ID claims, and Creative Commons tracks require you to credit the artist in the video description. It’s built for YouTube uploads; YouTube doesn’t say the tracks are cleared anywhere else, so check the license before you post one on another platform.
  • Instrumental and classical recordings with clear licenses. The compositions by Pachelbel, Debussy and Satie are in the public domain, but a specific recording can still be protected, so use one that comes with a license.
  • The platform’s own music picker. Adding a song inside Instagram or TikTok when you post is a different route from uploading a file with a commercial song mixed in; check each platform’s rules for your account type.

FAQ

What is a good song for a wedding slideshow?

A good slideshow song fits the photos on screen and has a lyric you’ve heard all the way through. Reliable picks: “In My Life” by The Beatles for childhood photos, “Lucky” by Jason Mraz and Colbie Caillat for dating, “Lean on Me” by Bill Withers for friends, and “What a Wonderful World” by Louis Armstrong to close. Choose songs that mean something to the two of you over whatever is trending.

How many songs should a wedding slideshow have?

One song for every 3 to 4 minutes of slideshow. A 5-minute sit-down slideshow usually needs one or two songs, a 7-minute one needs two, and a 10-minute rehearsal dinner slideshow needs about three. Change songs at a chapter break so the new music signals the next part of the story, and fade between them rather than cutting.

Can I use copyrighted songs in a wedding slideshow?

For the room, ask your DJ or venue coordinator how they handle music. Online is where problems start: platforms match uploads against registered songs, and a slideshow with a commercial track can be blocked, muted or have ads placed on it. Keep the full-music version for the reception and post a version with royalty-free music or a song added through the platform’s own music picker.

What song should end a wedding slideshow?

Pick something big and warm with a clear ending, so the last photo can hold while the music fades. “What a Wonderful World” by Louis Armstrong, “Home” by Phillip Phillips and “Thinking Out Loud” by Ed Sheeran are dependable closers. End on photos of the guests in the room, then hold the final image for 8 to 10 seconds before the screen goes dark.

Should a wedding slideshow use instrumental music?

Use instrumental music when words would compete with something else: captions, voice notes, a speech that leads into the slideshow, or a loop playing quietly under dinner conversation. For a short sit-down showing, a song with lyrics usually lands harder. You can also mix both, with an instrumental under the childhood captions and a lyric song for the finale.

Start with the YouTube Audio Library, which is free in YouTube Studio and marks which tracks need an artist credit. Paid stock-music services sell licenses for online video if you want more choice.
